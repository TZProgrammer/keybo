C. WHAT CHANGED — each claim CHECKED against the two tables above
========================================================================================

1. THE BOTTOM-ROW STORY: shattered -> ONE monotone column.
   SERVED spreads it over 3 one-hot columns with MIXED signs: bottom +0.7453  top -0.0459  home +0.0142
   INTERP puts it in one signed, monotone-constrained column: bottom_bias +0.5798

2. THE CONSTANT-COLUMN ARTIFACT: present -> GONE by construction.
   SERVED credits wpm -0.0922 ms/char (-9.2% of the gap) to a column that does not VARY at a fixed scoring WPM.
   INTERP has no wpm column at all, so the artifact is impossible by construction.

3. THE WRONG-SIGNED PHYSICAL STORY: distance -> same_hand_travel.
   SERVED: distance +0.0045 and dx +0.1678 — two mutually dependent columns (|r| = 0.9813) whose individual credits are not unique, and whose learned response prices LONG travel CHEAPER because long travel proxies for CROSS-HAND.
   INTERP: same_hand_travel +0.1252 — the same quantity CONDITIONED on same-hand and monotone-constrained, so 'farther is slower' is true of the fitted surface, verified (SHAP rho +0.8706).

4. NAME COLLISIONS: resolved.
   SERVED 'lateral' -0.1362 is an off-home COLUMN flag, not the `lat-span` GAUGE; SERVED 'inwards'/'outwards' (-0.0004/+0.0236) are SWAP-INVARIANT and so are not directions of travel at all.
   INTERP: off_home_column -0.0527 says what it measures; lateral_span +0.1116 IS the gauge's own predicate; roll_inward +0.1420 is antisymmetric under reversal, i.e. an actual direction.

5. DEAD COLUMNS: the served table's tail carries names that explain nothing.
   SERVED has 3 columns under 1e-3 ms/char: ['inwards', 'lsb', 'adjacent']
   INTERP has 0 — every column earns its place or is not in the frame.

6. THE BLOCK TABLE and the COLUMN table now say nearly the same thing.
   SERVED blocks (widest 6 columns): ROW +0.7136/3c  GEOMETRY +0.3081/6c  WPM -0.0922/1c  RELATIONAL +0.0910/5c  FINGER -0.0225/5c

   Blocks exist BECAUSE column credit is not unique across correlated columns. The narrower
   the widest block, the less the primary table has to hide — which is the whole claim.
